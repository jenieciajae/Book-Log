Men Who Hate Women This was such a good read! it’s both eye-opening and deeply important. Bates goes beyond surface-level discussions of misogyny, using undercover research to expose how online communities like incels, pickup artists, and men’s rights activists actively radicalize men and normalize hatred toward women. I think what makes this book so powerful is how clearly it connects digital misogyny to real-world consequences, from politics to violence, showing that this isn’t just “internet culture,” but a growing social threat. It’s educational without being overwhelming and motivating without being preachy. I genuinely believe more people need to read this, because awareness is the first step in recognizing and challenging these harmful ideologies. 9/10
Book Lovers This one truly lives up to its name — it’s a book for book lovers about book lovers. The story leans into publishing-world charm and literary references in a way that feels self-aware and fun rather than overdone. It’s a light, easy read that still has sharp, thoughtful writing and strong character dynamics. While it has its romantic moments, it never feels overly cheesy, and the chemistry feels grounded instead of forced. Overall, it’s clever, comforting, and perfect if you want something enjoyable that still feels well written. 8.6/10
